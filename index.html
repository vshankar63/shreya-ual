About me

I’m an award management and research project professional who helps externally funded work get delivered well, on time and in line with funder expectations. For almost four years at the International Rescue Committee, I’ve supported the full lifecycle of complex, multi-partner awards, from proposal development and budget-building through to compliance, reporting and close-out, across portfolios involving funders including FCDO, the European Commission (DG INTPA), Irish Aid, Sida, SDC, Danish MoFA and the Disaster Emergency Committee. Before IRC, I coordinated a $1.2m CIFF-funded adolescent health programme in Rajasthan, managing four on-ground implementation partners and reaching 50,000 adolescents across 100 villages.

I’m most at home where research ambition meets compliance. Across my award management roles, I’ve built financial cases for cost extensions, run contract and budget variations end to end, carried out partner due diligence, and kept records audit-ready across portfolios involving up to eight funders at once. I enjoy being the person academics and project teams can rely on to translate complicated funder rules into clear, practical guidance, and I’ve developed the process guides and training materials to back that up. My MA in Development Studies (IDS, Sussex) and background in economics give me a research-literate lens, and I care about how evidence becomes impact.

Research programme experience: ERICC

In mid-2024 I joined ERICC, the Education Research in Conflict and Crises Consortium, a £31.1m FCDO-funded education research programme delivered by IRC and eight research partners across seven countries, as Interim Senior Research Programme Coordinator. The programme was more than two years in, entering its penultimate quarter with a mid-programme leadership transition under way and a three-year cost extension on the line.

The immediate priority was donor compliance. I wrote the FY24 Q1 consortium narrative report and logframe for FCDO, consolidating and quality-assuring inputs from eight partners and country teams, alongside the Payment by Results documentation, and submitted on time. Alongside reporting, I established and maintained the governance and monitoring structures that held the consortium together: the MEAL Working Group, partner and consortium logframes, a RAG-rated deliverables tracker, risk-tracking inputs, and a programme workplan aligned to the costed workplan, developed with Finance and Programme Advisors.

I was the primary point of contact for eight research partners and seven country research managers, running regular check-ins, monitoring research progress, reviewing and approving partner quarterly reports, and resolving delivery issues directly. I acted as the FCDO focal point for MEAL strategy and reporting, supported monthly donor meetings with agendas and presentations, and kept partners informed of funder communications and requirement changes. I also supervised the Programme Manager, overseeing meeting planning, document management and milestone tracking.

On the research side, I coordinated publication and event timelines with the Communications and Knowledge Management leads, documented consortium learning through reflection sessions, and supported the Annual All-Consortium Meeting in Amman. One of ERICC’s key partners was UCL; working closely with their team on research deliverables, alongside my MA from the Institute of Development Studies, gave me grounded experience of how research gets produced and disseminated in a university setting.

How I see this role

North star

Award management that researchers can rely on, and funders can trust.

The challenge

UAL’s research and knowledge exchange is ambitious, often international, and often multi-partner. The funders behind it, from Horizon Europe and UKRI to Innovate UK and EuropeAid, each have their own rules, systems and audit expectations. The project manager’s job is to absorb that complexity so it never slows the research down.

Who this role serves

  • Academics: clear guidance before they apply, no surprises after award, and more time for research.
  • Funders and partners: accurate, timely reporting, audit-ready records, and one reliable point of contact.
  • UAL and RKEE: compliant projects, budgets that are regularly re-forecast, and more follow-on funding and impact from every award.

Likely barriers, and how I’d approach them

“Compliance feels like bureaucracy.”

I translate funder rules into short, practical guidance, explain why each rule exists, and only ask for what’s needed.

“Creative practice doesn’t always fit funder templates.”

I work with academics early on impact pathways and dissemination, so the application tells their story in the funder’s language.

These are hypotheses from the outside. I’d test them in my first 30 days.

My first 90 days

Days 1–30

Listen and map

  • Meet the EU R&I Funding Team, RKEE colleagues, RMA staff and College-based research support leads; attend a researcher meeting to understand how academics engage with the centre.
  • Map the active Horizon Europe and UKRI portfolio: funder conditions, reporting cycles, amendment deadlines, payment claims and live risks.
  • Get hands-on with Worktribe, Agresso, Symplectic Elements, SyGMA and the EU Funding & Tenders Portal; shadow a pre-award enquiry through to submission readiness.
  • Understand UAL’s internal approval and governance processes for new applications and contract variations.
Days 31–60

Take ownership

  • Own my first post-award reporting cycle, financial and narrative, for at least one funder, submitted accurately and on time.
  • Support a live pre-award submission: draft or review budget, coordinate consortium partner inputs, check eligibility and funder compliance requirements.
  • Build a consolidated portfolio view: compliance requirements, upcoming reporting deadlines, open risks and amendment triggers across all active awards.
  • Begin regular one-to-ones with academic PIs on active grants to understand project progress and surface any emerging issues early.
Days 61–90

Contribute and improve

  • Lead a project management meeting with an academic PI, putting in place a shared tracking rhythm for their grant.
  • Draft a short pre-award readiness guide for UAL’s most common funders (Horizon Europe, UKRI), refined with the team and tested with a researcher.
  • Identify and flag any compliance gaps or reporting risks spotted during portfolio mapping, and agree a plan to close them.
  • Agree a regular budget re-forecasting cadence with the finance team for the projects in my portfolio.

Principle: understand first, take ownership early, then build improvements that last.
